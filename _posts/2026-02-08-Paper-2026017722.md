## Overview

Designing a realistic haptic texture usually means tuning low-level signal parameters by trial and error. This project replaces that with language: type what a surface should feel like, such as "gritty but cushioned" or "smooth hard metal", and immediately see and feel the result through a 3D haptic stylus.

A single prompt generates three coordinated outputs: sliding vibrations that respond to how hard and fast you move, tapping transients that convey hardness, and a matching visual preview. A shared, language-aligned latent space keeps what you feel consistent with what you typed and what you see.

## Highlights

- One prompt drives both haptic channels and a visual preview.
- A shared, language-aligned latent space keeps the haptic and visual output semantically consistent.
- User studies show that prompts give perceptually meaningful control over roughness, hardness, and slipperiness.

## How It Works

![Language-guided texture authoring system overview](/img/LangTexture_system.png)

- **Text to haptic latent.** A frozen CLIP text encoder and a lightweight projection head map the prompt to a 64-dimensional haptic latent.
- **Two haptic decoders.** One decoder outputs a *tap bank* (acceleration transients at 13 impact speeds) for hardness cues. The other outputs an *AR matrix*: autoregressive vibration models over an 18-point force–speed grid for sliding texture.
- **Training.** The model is trained on the 100 measured materials of the Penn Haptic Texture Toolkit, augmented to 2,000 training pairs, with five LLM-written captions per material. The loss combines reconstruction, KL regularization, and a contrastive (InfoNCE) term that aligns the latent with the text embedding.
- **Visual preview.** An off-the-shelf Stable Diffusion 2 pipeline turns the same prompt into a texture image. It is decoupled from the haptic latent, so the two channels are linked through the prompt rather than through shared weights.
- **Rendering.** A 3D Systems Touch device runs at 1 kHz. Tap responses are interpolated linearly over impact speed, and AR models are interpolated barycentrically over the user's current force and speed.

## Results

- **An organized material space.** The learned latent space groups the 100 training materials cleanly, in close agreement with their labels.
- **Blends that make sense.** In a study with 17 people, participants rated textures generated between two real materials. Their ratings followed consistent trends: roughness tracked surface bumps, hardness tracked compliance, and slipperiness tracked surface films.
- **Low-effort authoring.** People found prompt-based iteration easy, felt that the haptic and visual outputs fit together, and agreed that the textures matched their descriptions.

This model is also the text-to-haptics engine behind [TouchTwin]({% post_url 2026-09-27-TouchTwin %}), which applies it to entire 3D-scanned scenes.

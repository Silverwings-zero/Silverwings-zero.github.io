## Abstract

Authoring realistic haptic textures often requires low-level parameter tuning and repeated trial-and-error, which slows iteration and limits creative exploration. This work introduces a language-driven authoring system that turns natural-language prompts into multimodal textures: coordinated haptic signals (sliding vibrations generated by force/speed-conditioned autoregressive models and tapping transients) and a text-prompted visual preview from a diffusion model. A shared, language-aligned latent space links modalities so a single prompt yields semantically consistent haptic and visual output; designers can specify goals like “gritty but cushioned” or “smooth hard metal” and immediately see and feel the result through a 3D haptic device. An anchor-referenced, attribute-wise evaluation for roughness, slipperiness, and hardness shows perceptually meaningful structure in the latent, with trends reflecting asperity, compliance, and surface-film effects. A human-subject study further indicates coherent cross-modal experience and low effort for prompt-based iteration. Overall, the results show language as a practical control modality for texture authoring, enabling prompt-first, designer-oriented workflows that replace manual parameter tuning with interpretable, text-guided refinement.

## Contributions

- Language-driven multimodal texture authoring that unifies haptic signals and visual previews from a single prompt.
- A shared, language-aligned latent space that preserves semantic consistency across haptic and visual channels.
- User studies and attribute-wise evaluations demonstrating perceptually meaningful control over roughness, hardness, and slipperiness.

## How It Works

![Language-guided texture authoring system overview](/img/LangTexture_system.png)

- **Text to haptic latent.** A frozen CLIP text encoder and a lightweight projection head map the prompt to a 64-dimensional haptic latent.
- **Two haptic decoders.** One decoder outputs a *tap bank* (acceleration transients at 13 impact speeds) for hardness cues. The other outputs an *AR matrix*: autoregressive vibration models over an 18-point force–speed grid for sliding texture.
- **Training.** The model is trained on the 100 measured materials of the Penn Haptic Texture Toolkit, augmented to 2,000 training pairs, with five LLM-written captions per material. The loss combines reconstruction, KL regularization, and a contrastive (InfoNCE) term that aligns the latent with the text embedding.
- **Visual preview.** An off-the-shelf Stable Diffusion 2 pipeline turns the same prompt into a texture image. It is decoupled from the haptic latent, so the two channels are linked through the prompt rather than through shared weights.
- **Rendering.** A 3D Systems Touch device runs at 1 kHz. Tap responses are interpolated linearly over impact speed, and AR models are interpolated barycentrically over the user's current force and speed.

## Evaluation

- **Latent structure.** The latent clusters closely match the 100 material classes (silhouette ≈ 0.96, adjusted Rand index ≈ 0.97, NMI ≈ 0.99).
- **Anchor-referenced perception (N = 17).** Participants rated textures generated from interpolations between two real materials. Their ratings of roughness, slipperiness, and hardness were projected onto the line between those two anchors. The resulting trends are consistent and interpretable: asperity drives roughness, compliance drives hardness, and surface films drive slipperiness.
- **Authoring usability.** Participants reported a coherent cross-modal experience, low workload (NASA-TLX), and agreed that the generated textures matched their descriptions.

This model is also the text-to-haptics engine behind [TouchTwin]({% post_url 2026-09-27-TouchTwin %}), which applies it to entire 3D-scanned scenes.
